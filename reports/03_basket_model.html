03 · The basket model: what is probably in the trolley but not on the scan list?¶

A partial rescan today checks a few units picked at random. This model tells the colleague which product types to count instead.

Two kinds of missing item matter almost equally:

  • a product type the shopper never scanned (forgot the steak; skipped the wine)
  • a product type they scanned too few of (scanned 2 of 3 yogurts)

So the model scores every product type (commodity) in a session: the probability that it holds units in the bags that are not on the scan list. Times its typical Nectar price, that is the value a check of it is expected to recover.

Masked-basket training. Staffed-till baskets are complete. Hide 10% of their units at random, and train the model to say where the hidden units were, from the visible rest and the member's Nectar history (earlier trips only; for SmartShop trips, only what was scanned). It never sees a theft label: it learns what should be in the trolley, not who steals.

3. A fair test: same effort, same detection rules¶

Every TEST-period SmartShop session where something left unscanned (hidden truth). Each method gets the same budget of units a colleague may count per session; it walks down its list and counts each product type that still fits. Detection follows the simulator for every method: mistakes are found when their type is counted; units skipped on purpose are usually concealed outside the bags, so any partial check finds them only rarely.

Why the gain is modest, and why that is a finding. Genuine mistakes fall roughly at random across the units in the bags, so picking random units already samples where they are. A model can only add what random picks cannot see: prices, multi-buys and what was scanned. And no partial check, however targeted, finds items concealed outside the bags. Catching deliberate non-scanning is a question of who gets a full rescan: the risk model and rescan policy (weeks 5–6).

4. Is "this basket looks incomplete" a risk signal?¶

The idea from the outline: the model's total expected missing value for a session, used to flag risky sessions. Tested on a random sample of test sessions, with and without missing items.

In [6]:
lo, hi = TEST_DAYS
sample = con.sql(f'''
SELECT s.basket_id, t.gt_deliberate_value > 0 AS deliberate, t.gt_nonscan_units > 0 AS any_missing
FROM sim_obs.sessions s JOIN sim_truth.sessions t USING (basket_id)
WHERE s.day BETWEEN {lo} AND {hi}
''').df().sample(6000, random_state=0)
gap = score_sessions(con, model, sample.basket_id).groupby('basket_id').agg(
    gap_value=('exp_value', 'sum'), scanned_units=('n_vis_units', 'first'))
d = sample.merge(gap, left_on='basket_id', right_index=True)
d['gap_per_unit'] = d.gap_value / (d.scanned_units + 1)
pd.DataFrame({
    f: [roc_auc_score(d.deliberate, d[f]), roc_auc_score(d.any_missing, d[f])]
    for f in ['gap_value', 'gap_per_unit', 'scanned_units']
}, index=['AUC: deliberate session', 'AUC: anything missing']).round(3)

The total gap mostly restates basket size (bigger baskets have more to miss); per scanned unit it carries a little signal for deliberate sessions. It goes into the week 5 risk model as one feature among many, not as a detector on its own.

6. One check list¶

A colleague's screen for one randomly chosen test session: what was scanned, the three types to count, and (hidden truth) what was really missing.

miss = truth[truth.basket_id.isin(ids)].groupby('basket_id').miss_value.sum()
example = miss[miss.between(5, 15)].sample(1, random_state=7).index[0]
s = scored[scored.basket_id == example]
s = s.assign(priority=s.exp_value / (s.vis_units.clip(lower=1) + s.p_missing))
scanned = s[s.vis_units > 0].sort_values('vis_units', ascending=False)
print('Scanned:', ', '.join(f"{r.commodity.title()} x{int(r.vis_units)}" for r in scanned.itertuples()))
t = truth[truth.basket_id == example].set_index('commodity')
print('Really missing:', ', '.join(f"{c.title()} x{int(n)}" for c, n in t.miss_units.items()))
top = s.nlargest(3, 'priority')[['commodity', 'vis_units', 'p_missing', 'exp_value']]

Findings¶

# Finding Implication
1 About half of missing value is short counts of a scanned type, half types never scanned The model scores every product type, scanned or not
2 Masked-basket model: held-out AUC 0.93 and calibrated (mean prediction 0.26% vs 0.25% actual), trained without any theft label "What should be in this trolley", fair by design
3 At the same budget, its check list finds as many honest mistakes as today's random units while counting about 25% fewer units (1.3–1.5x found per unit counted) Faster, friendlier partial rescans for honest shoppers
4 Simple rules do worse than random: "check the most valuable scanned items" finds about a third as much per unit Intuition is not enough; measure against random
5 No partial check finds much deliberate loss (concealed items), and the basket gap is a weak risk signal on its own Theft is caught by choosing who gets a full rescan (weeks 5–6), not by item lists

Limit. The simulator spreads genuine mistakes evenly across units (apart from multi-buys and basket size). If real mistakes cluster on particular products (bulky items, loose produce, multi-buy promotions), targeted lists gain more; the random floor of full rescans would show it.
