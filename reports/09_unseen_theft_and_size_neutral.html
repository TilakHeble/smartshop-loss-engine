09 · An unseen theft method, and closing the age gap (policy v1.1)¶

Two questions a reviewer would ask after week 10:

  1. "Your model only finds the signals you put in the simulator." Test it on a theft method the model was never designed around: barcode switching, where a shopper scans a cheap item's barcode for an expensive one. The scan count matches the basket, the pace is normal and nothing is deleted, so the strongest signals disappear.
  2. The age gap (week 10): younger family households were stopped about 1.6x as often as the oldest when honest, because their baskets are bigger. Can it be fixed, and at what cost?

Produced by python -m smartshop.experiments.sensitivity and python -m smartshop.reporting.mitigation; this notebook reads their outputs.

1. Barcode switching¶

Each switching world reruns the whole chain twice:

  • model retrained: the store keeps learning from its random full rescans, as the weekly job does;
  • model not retrained: the model and policy shape built for the world without switching, with only rescan volume reset to colleague time (a new method appears after deployment).

"Deliberate loss in the top 12% of scores" asks whether the risk ranking still points at theft (random would be 12%).

Reading it.

  • The policy still beats today in every case. Even when every thief switches barcodes and the model is never retrained, loss is lower, recovery is higher and honest stops fall by the same ~69%.
  • The gain shrinks, as it should: the scan-log signals the model relied on are gone for switchers. What is left is member history: switchers' baskets hold fewer high-risk items than their usual Nectar baskets, so the ranking still points at theft well above chance.
  • Retraining helps a little. Random full rescans keep finding switched items, so the weekly retrain learns from them.
  • The random floor is the safety net. It catches switched items at the same rate as anything else, so total loss stays measurable without bias, and a rise in losses found on low-score sessions is the early warning that a new method has appeared.

2. Closing the age gap: compare like with like¶

The risk model learns "will a rescan find anything". Big baskets nearly always hold an honest slip, so raw scores favour them, and they are also the slowest to rescan. Younger family households buy the biggest baskets, hence the gap.

The fix: compare each session only with sessions of a similar size (its rank within a band of units scanned, mapped back onto the overall score distribution), blended with the raw score at a chosen strength. It uses only units scanned, which the store sees; no demographics. The strength was chosen on the tuning period (days 331–500); the table is the test period, run as the policy would operate (shoppers respond, trust over four cycles, 10 runs).

Why it catches more, not less. Rescans move away from slow 40+ item baskets full of honest slips towards sessions that look unusual for their size. Each rescan is quicker, so more fit in the same colleague time, and a larger share land on deliberate non-scanning. The cost is more honest shoppers stopped than v1.0 (though still far fewer than today), and at full strength new SmartShop users reach the edge of the four-fifths rule. At 0.5 the age ratio rises from 0.61 to 0.69, which the permutation test can no longer tell apart from chance for groups this small. Strength 0.5 balances the three and becomes policy v1.1: served by the API, recalibrated weekly, and shown on the dashboard.

Findings¶

# Finding Implication
1 Barcode switching, a method the model was not built for, weakens the gain but never reverses it: even with every thief switching and no retraining, the policy beats today on loss, recovery and honest stops The result does not rest only on the signals the simulator planted
2 Member history keeps the ranking pointing at theft when scan-log signals vanish; retraining on random full rescans recovers part of the gap Keep the floor and the weekly retrain; watch finds on low-score sessions as an early warning
3 Scoring each session against others of a similar size narrows the age gap (0.61 to 0.69 at strength 0.5, no longer distinguishable from chance; 0.90 at full strength) and cuts loss further at the same colleague time, for more honest stops than v1.0 Policy v1.1 (strength 0.5)
4 The gain comes from cost: big baskets are slow to rescan and mostly hold honest slips The v1.0 score answered "will a rescan find anything", not "is this rescan worth a colleague's time"
