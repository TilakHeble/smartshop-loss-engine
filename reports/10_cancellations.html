10 · Cancelled sessions: genuine ones, and cancelling to dodge a rescan (v1.2)¶

A shopper can cancel SmartShop part-way through and pay at a staffed till. Usually that is ordinary: a flat battery, an app crash, a change of mind. But it can also be a way round the policy: told "you've been selected for a rescan", a shopper cancels and pays at a till for what they put on the belt, keeping whatever they hid. If cancelling carries no consequence:

  • any rescan policy can be dodged, and the better it picks out thieves, the more of the people it asks have something to hide;
  • loss measured from the rescans that were completed misses exactly the sessions most likely to hold it.

Two sets of rules are compared, for today's random rescans and the v1.1 policy, at the same colleague time:

Today's rules (open) Closed rules (v1.2)
Cancel part-way Pay at a till; no exit decision Treated as an exit: the same decision
Selected, then cancel Pay at a till; the rescan is skipped The session is locked; a colleague completes the rescan
Crash or flat battery logged by the app As above Random floor (2%) only, so a genuine failure is never singled out
Would-be dodgers under the lock — Some abandon the trolley and leave (their hidden items are lost)

At a staffed till everything on the belt is scanned, so honest mistakes are paid for; only items deliberately hidden are lost. Shoppers who steal respond to the rescan chance they effectively face, so an open loophole also invites more theft. No published figure exists for how often people cancel or dodge, so the base values are stated assumptions (3% of sessions cancelled genuinely, 40% of them logged by the app; 40–60% of selected shoppers with hidden items dodge; 15% walk away under the lock), and six other worlds vary them.

Produced by python -m smartshop.reporting.cancellations; this notebook reads its outputs.

Reading it.

  • Under today's rules, v1.1 still beats today's random rescans, but its gain shrinks from about 20% to about 11%. The loophole hurts the risk-based policy more than it hurts random rescans: the better a policy picks out thieves, the more of the shoppers it asks have something to hide, and they are the ones who dodge. Hidden loss kept by dodgers is about twice as large under v1.1 as under today's random rescans.
  • Closing the loophole brings v1.1 back to about 20% less loss than today, close to the no-cancellation reference; what remains is shoppers who abandon the trolley and leave.
  • An open loophole also invites more theft: adaptive shoppers steal more often when a rescan can be dodged.

Under closed rules a genuine canceller is asked for a rescan no more often than any honest shopper who finishes: a cancellation the app logs as a crash or flat battery gets only the random floor, and the rest get the same decision as at the exit. Nobody is flagged for cancelling, and mid-shop cancellations are never used as a risk signal. The crash flag must come from the app's own logs, never from what the shopper says, or it becomes the new loophole.

The inverse-probability estimate from week 8 assumes every requested rescan happens. When selected shoppers can cancel, the missing rescans are not random: they are the shoppers with something hidden. Under today's rules the estimate misses over a third of the loss; with the lock the remaining bias comes only from those who walk away, and it is visible: every walk-away after a rescan request is logged, so the store can report it next to the loss estimate.

Findings¶

# Finding Implication
1 If a selected shopper can cancel and pay at a till, v1.1's gain falls from about 20% to about 11% less loss than today The loophole matters more than any modelling choice since week 8
2 It hurts the risk-based policy more than random rescans: the better the policy targets, the more of those it asks have something to hide Close the loophole before rolling out targeting
3 Closed rules (lock once selected; a cancellation is an exit; app-logged crashes get the floor) restore about 20% less loss, and it holds in all seven worlds tested v1.2 rules
4 Genuine cancellers are asked for a rescan no more often than other honest shoppers; nobody is flagged for cancelling Fair to people with a flat battery
5 Dodged rescans bias the loss estimate by about −38%; with the lock the bias falls to about −6%, from walk-aways the store can count Report walk-aways next to the weighted loss estimate
