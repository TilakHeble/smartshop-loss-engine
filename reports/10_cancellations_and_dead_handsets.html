10 · Sessions that never reach the till: cancellations, cancel-and-walk, dead handsets (v1.5)¶

Everything else assumes a SmartShop session ends at the till barcode, where the check decision is made. In store, some never get there:

  • Genuine cancellation. A shopper scans a few items (or most of them), gets an urgent call or can't find what they came for, cancels SmartShop and leaves the items. Nothing is lost.
  • Cancel-and-walk. A shoplifter scans items so the session looks normal, cancels, pays nothing, and leaves through the self-scan gates behind a customer who has scanned their receipt barcode to open them, which is easier when it is busy. The whole basket is lost, and no check ever sees it.
  • Dead device. The store's handset (or the shopper's phone) dies part-way. Today the customer has to scan everything again. A handset is the store's equipment, so that is the store's failure, not the customer's.

Genuine cancellations and cancel-and-walk can both happen early or late, so timing does not tell them apart.

What the store has. Colleagues carry no device. The SmartShop check device at the SmartShop checkout logs in and scans the barcode a shopper gets for a check. Customers pick their own handset from the stand, or use their phone, and start with their Nectar card. (v1.3 assumed a colleague could get a prompt and that the store hands out handsets; neither is true, so v1.5 replaces both.)

The handsets show no battery level and customers pick their own, but the store knows which handset each session used and when it went back on the stand. Replacing the battery of any handset that dies twice in 30 days (A) removes most dead handsets, because a few worn batteries cause most of them. B (a handset back on the stand for less than about 90 minutes asks the customer to take another) helps on its own but adds little on top of A. When one still dies, D resumes the session instead of making the customer rescan everything.

Reading it.

  • Nothing at the moment of cancelling stops a walk-out once colleagues get no prompt: in the base world every arm loses the same. Gates that flag two people per receipt scan (idea 3, hardware) cut it by about a quarter whatever else is done.
  • Idea 1 still earns its place. About 70% of genuine cancellers (an assumption) leave the basket at the point and are helped, and cancel-and-walk becomes countable: about 3 in 10 unfinished cancels are walk-outs, a loss nobody can see today.
  • Idea 2, one strike, is too blunt: most of those full checks land on honest shoppers who forgot to hand the basket back (about 15% of honest members over the period).
  • Idea 2, two in 90 days, is the rule to use: about 2% of honest members, and it recovers about $154 per colleague hour from the members it does check, about twice the targeted policy's rate.
  • Whether idea 2 deters walk-outs is the open question. If it puts off a quarter of attempts, cancel-and-walk falls by about a quarter; if half, by half. A trial should measure it.

Under the two-in-90-days rule only 1–3% of honest members in any group are checked this way, so a handful of members moves the group ratios a lot: too few to read as a gap, and worth monitoring in a trial. Cancelling is never used as a risk-model feature, and a device failure is never a signal.

Findings¶

# Finding Implication
1 Cancel-and-walk is about an eighth of all loss and invisible to checks and to the loss estimate Count unfinished cancels next to the weighted loss estimate
2 Without a colleague device, nothing at the moment of cancelling stops a walk-out; finishing cancels at the SmartShop point helps genuine shoppers and makes walk-outs countable (3 in 10 unfinished cancels) v1.5 idea 1
3 A full check after one unfinished cancel mostly checks honest shoppers; after two in 90 days it reaches about 2% of honest members and recovers about $154 per colleague hour v1.5 idea 2: two in 90 days
4 Walk-outs fall only if idea 2 deters them (unmeasured); gates that allow one person per receipt cut them by a quarter regardless Measure deterrence in a trial; recommend the gates
5 Replacing batteries that keep dying cuts dead handsets by about 93%, from the handset log alone; the "take another" screen adds little A first; resume (D) for the rest
