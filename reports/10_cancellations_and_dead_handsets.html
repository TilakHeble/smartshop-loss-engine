10 · Sessions that never reach the till: cancellations, cancel-and-walk, dead handsets (v1.3)¶

Everything up to v1.1 assumed every SmartShop session ends at the till barcode, where the rescan decision is made. In store, some never get there:

  • Genuine cancellation. A shopper scans a few items (or most of them), gets an urgent call or can't find what they came for, cancels SmartShop and leaves the items. Nothing is lost.
  • Cancel-and-walk. A shoplifter scans items so the session looks normal, cancels, pays nothing, and leaves through the self-scan gates behind a customer who has scanned their receipt barcode to open them, which is easier when it is busy. The whole basket is lost, and no rescan ever sees it.
  • Dead device. The store's handset (or the shopper's phone) dies part-way. Today the customer has to scan everything again on another handset, at self-scan or at a checkout. A handset is the store's equipment, so that is the store's failure, not the customer's.

Genuine cancellations and cancel-and-walk can both happen early or late in a shop, so timing does not tell them apart. Any response has to be one that is right for both.

v1.3:

Today v1.3
Cancelled with items scanned Nothing happens A colleague near the exit gets a prompt to help (take the basket back, find the item, show the way to a till); every cancellation, or only the higher-risk ones when colleagues are stretched
Handset or phone dies Customer rescans everything Resume on another handset or at a till from the Nectar card; never a risk signal
Handsets handed out Whichever the customer grabs Longest on the rack first, and handsets that keep dying sent for a new battery, from the handset log alone (the handsets show no battery level)

No published figures exist for any of these rates, so every one is a stated assumption (in policy/exits.py and ops/handsets.py) and eight other worlds vary them. Produced by python -m smartshop.reporting.exits; this notebook reads its outputs.

The handsets show no battery level, so the store cannot choose by charge. But it knows which handset each session used and when it went back on the rack. Handing out the handset that has been charging longest avoids most deaths of handsets put back half-empty, and sending handsets that die twice in a month for a new battery removes the worn ones. Together dead handsets fall by 92% in this fleet and by 72–93% across five other simulated fleets. When one still dies, v1.3 resumes the session instead of making the customer rescan everything.

Reading it.

  • Cancel-and-walk is a blind spot today: about an eighth of all loss, and no rescan, model or loss estimate ever sees it.
  • A help prompt on cancellation halves it when every cancellation gets one, and cuts it by about two-fifths when only the higher-risk half do. It stays effective in every world, and pays far more per colleague hour than rescans do ($75 per hour for v1.1), even counting the time resume saves on dead devices.
  • Most prompts go to genuine shoppers. Only about one answered prompt in ten is a walk-out (one in eight with the risk score). That is why it must be help, worded and trained as service, with no reasons shown to the colleague, and why "help every cancellation" is also simply good service.
  • The risk score earns its keep when colleagues are stretched: prompting only higher-risk cancellations keeps most of the effect for about half the colleague time.

Among genuine cancellers, the share who would get a "higher-risk" prompt is similar across every group (four-fifths ratios 0.85 or above), and in any case a prompt is an offer of help, not a check. A device failure is never a risk signal, and cancelling is never used as one.

Findings¶

# Finding Implication
1 Cancel-and-walk is about an eighth of all loss and invisible to rescans and to the loss estimate Count it separately (prompt and gate logs) next to the weighted loss estimate
2 A colleague prompt to help on every cancellation halves it; prompting only higher-risk cancellations cuts it by about two-fifths for about half the colleague time; both hold in every world v1.3: help every cancellation when a colleague is near the exit, higher-risk only when stretched
3 About one answered prompt in ten is a walk-out (one in eight for higher-risk prompts) It has to be help, never an accusation
4 Handing out the longest-charged handset and replacing worn batteries cuts dead handsets by 72–93% across simulated fleets, from the handset log alone Cheap, and it is the store's problem to fix
5 Resume after a dead device saves about 10 customer hours and 3 colleague hours a period in the simulated chain Never make a customer rescan for the store's failure
