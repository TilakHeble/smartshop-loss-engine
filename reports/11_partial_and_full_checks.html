11 · Partial checks that escalate, and full checks (v1.4)¶

When a SmartShop shopper scans the till barcode to pay, the till can ask for one of two checks, and a colleague carries it out:

  • Partial check: the colleague checks a sample of the basket, about 20–25% of the items (at least 2). If anything is unscanned, the check device suggests a full check of the whole basket.
  • Full check: every item, straight away.

Up to v1.3 the project modelled a partial check as a fixed 3 items that stopped there, whatever the basket size. v1.4 models it as above, recalibrated so partial checks still find something about as often as published research reports (2.88% of audits, ECR; simulated 3.0%).

Two questions:

  1. How much does modelling today's checks properly change the comparison?
  2. When the till targets a shopper, should it ask for a partial check (cheaper, so more shoppers can be checked) or a full check? The share of risk-based checks that are full is chosen on the tuning period, at today's colleague time and on a half-staffed shift.

Produced by python -m smartshop.reporting.checks; this notebook reads its outputs.

1. Today's checks, modelled properly¶

Partial checks that grow with the basket and escalate find more than 3 fixed items did: today recovers about 43% more than earlier versions credited it with, and takes about 18% more colleague time (bigger samples, plus the full checks they escalate to). The recommended policy is given that same, larger colleague time. Its advantage on recovery shrinks (5.5x rather than 7.1x), and it makes more checks, so it stops a few more honest shoppers than in v1.3 (still 58% fewer than today). It cuts loss by about a fifth, as before.

Reading it.

  • Turning targeted checks into partial checks always adds loss. At today's colleague time, making every risk-based check partial leaves about 12% more loss than making them all full; it only stops getting worse once at least the top tenth of shoppers by risk get full checks, and those are where all the targeted checks go anyway.
  • It holds when short-staffed, when cheaper partial checks might have been expected to win by covering more shoppers.
  • It holds if partial checks were more careful (finding 97% of picked mistakes rather than 53%) or checked 25% of the basket instead of 20% (checked separately while building v1.4).
  • Why: items taken on purpose are usually concealed, and a partial check rarely reaches them; it escalates only if the sample happens to contain a slip. A full check of a shopper the model has already picked out goes straight to what matters.

1 Modelled properly (20% of the basket, escalating), today's checks recover about 43% more than 3 fixed items did, for about 18% more colleague time Today is a stronger baseline; the headline is restated against it

3. How big should a partial check be?¶

A bigger sample finds more per check but takes longer, so at the same colleague time fewer shoppers can be checked: fewer honest shoppers stopped, but less deterrence. Every rule below is run with today's random checks (2% full, and as many partial checks as today's colleague time allows), on the tuning period, against shoppers who respond. Candidates: a fixed 3 or 5 items; one share for every basket (10%–75%, at least 2 items); and a share by basket size (up to 10 items / 11–30 / over 30, each 10%, 20%, 30% or 50%), 74 rules in all.

How much a partial check deters a would-be thief has never been measured, so three assumptions are tested: thieves fear any check alike; a partial check deters half as much as a full one; or it deters in proportion to the share of the basket it covers.

Reading it.

  • There is no single optimum without knowing how thieves see a partial check. If they fear any check alike, small samples win (10%: more checks, so more deterrence). If they know a quick sample rarely reaches hidden items, large samples win (50–75%). Either way loss moves by only 1–2% across sensible sizes at the same colleague time.
  • 25% of every basket is the robust choice: under every assumption its loss is within 1.4% of the best rule for that assumption. Against today's 20% it leaves loss unchanged (within ±0.3% on the test period), recovers about 5% more and stops about 6% fewer honest shoppers, at the same colleague time, because slightly fewer, slightly bigger checks are made.
  • Size bands do not help. The best rule by basket size (30% up to 30 items, 20% above) is 0.05 points better in the worst case than 25% for every basket: no difference worth explaining to colleagues. The minimum of 2 items already looks after small baskets.
  • It does not change the targeted-check finding: with the chosen sample size, a partial check for targeted shoppers still loses to a full one.
  • What would settle it: a store trial arm with 25% and one with 10%, measuring how often shoppers who would steal are deterred. Until then, 25%.
