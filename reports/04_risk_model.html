04 · The session risk model: who should get a full rescan?¶

Week 4 showed that partial checks, however well targeted, rarely find items skipped on purpose: they are concealed outside the bags. Catching deliberate non-scanning comes down to who gets a full rescan. This notebook builds the score that decides it.

The discipline. The model only learns from what a store can observe:

  • features: the session's scan log, and the member's history from earlier trips (Nectar purchases, earlier SmartShop sessions, earlier rescan outcomes). No demographics, no channel, no time of day, no tenure (see section 6 for why).
  • labels: what random full rescans found. Unbiased because selection was random, but scarce (2% of sessions). Partial rescans are not used: they rarely see concealed items.
  • selection: label, feature set and model chosen by repeated cross-validation of observable value capture ("of the value found by held-out random full rescans, how much would our top 20% have caught?"). The hidden truth is used once, at the end.

Different random draws of ~900 labels give quite different models (52–59% across seeds; the actual history gave 51%), so a 2% floor is noisy as well as small. More labels of the same kind help only a little on average. The ceiling is far higher because it is trained on why items were missing; a rescan only records that they were, and most findings are honest mistakes. Logging what kind of item was found (a high-risk line, say) is a cheap process change a store could test; with today's few labels it did not clearly help here.

6. Fairness preview¶

Being flagged is an adverse outcome. Raw flag rates can differ because groups contain different amounts of deliberate non-scanning, so the main check is the honest-shopper stop rate: among sessions with nothing skipped on purpose, how often each group is flagged (top 12%). A ratio below 0.8 between the lowest and highest group breaches the four-fifths rule of thumb. Demographics are used here only. Intervals resample members: a group's sessions come from few households.

What the first version of this check found, and what was fixed. The first model flagged handset users, evening shoppers and new SmartShop users far more often, although none of these changes behaviour in the simulation. With so few labels the model had learned spurious effects of channel and time of day, and tenure features (trips, sessions and rescans so far) made newcomers look unusual. All three were removed from scoring, and missing history is now filled with typical values, so a new user is scored as typical, not suspicious.

groups = {'channel': 'channel', 'new SmartShop user': 'new_smartshop_user', 'evening peak': 'is_peak',
          'age group': 'age_group', 'household size': 'household_size', 'income level': 'income_level'}
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

def stop_ratio(d, col):
    r = d.groupby(col).flag.mean()
    return r.min() / r.max()

rows = []
for label, col in groups.items():
    d = tt.dropna(subset=[col])
    d = d[~d.deliberate]
    big = d.groupby(col).size()
    d = d[d[col].isin(big[big >= 200].index)]
    members = d.member_id.unique()
    boot = [stop_ratio(pd.DataFrame({'member_id': rng.choice(members, len(members))}).merge(d, on='member_id'), col)
            for _ in range(300)]
    rows.append({'attribute': label, 'groups': d[col].nunique(),
                 'fewest households in a group': d.groupby(col).member_id.nunique().min(),
                 'honest-stop ratio': stop_ratio(d, col),
                 'CI low': np.percentile(boot, 2.5), 'CI high': np.percentile(boot, 97.5)})
fair = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index('attribute')

Findings¶

# Finding Implication
1 Only 939 random full rescans in the training period, 185 of which found anything Label scarcity is the binding constraint, as in a real store
2 Chosen on observable labels only: "found anything", session + history + basket gap, logistic regression; the leading candidates are close Honest selection; the truth played no part
3 Flagging 12% of sessions catches 51% of deliberately skipped value and 52% of net loss (random: 12%) About 4x today's random selection at the same rescan rate
4 Store-style rules: biggest baskets 21%, red-flag points 33%, best single signal (deletions) 48% of deliberate value and 42% of net loss Combining weak signals beats every rule, most clearly on total loss
5 A 5–20% random full floor adds only 2–5 points; a model trained on why items were missing reaches 82% The bottleneck is label quality (rescans record that, not why), more than label count
6 First fairness check failed on channel, time of day and new users; fixed by removing those features and tenure. Honest-stop gaps remain for age, household size and income Review items carried into the policy (per-member caps) and the full check (week 10)

Caveat. Signal strengths come from the simulator's assumptions (deletions, pauses, habits), softened so no single habit is a fingerprint. Week 8 tests conclusions under weaker signals.
